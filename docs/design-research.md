## Navigation and conversion

Seven explicit navigation links, a persistent resume action on wide screens, and a keyboard-operable mobile menu. The homepage introduces Julie, establishes capabilities, explains experience, presents services, and ends with two equal contact choices. Recruiters and clients receive equally prominent primary actions.

## Typography and visual hierarchy

Use a fast local sans-serif stack and small monospace labels. A large name, clear professional title, and supporting copy establish hierarchy. Deep navy surfaces, muted borders, and cyan accents create a technical identity without a dashboard full of invented status data.

## Skills, experience, and case studies

Group skills by business capability, with browser-local search and category filters. Use a vertical timeline to keep roles and exact dates understandable. Project pages will explain challenge, role, approach, verified outcomes, and tools only after approval. At launch, all five templates remain private and the project listing has a deliberate empty state.

## Responsiveness and accessibility

Stack layouts for narrow screens, keep controls reachable, provide semantic landmarks, a skip link, visible keyboard focus, accessible control names, and live filter feedback. Respect reduced motion. Keep biography and experience readable without hover. Treat reference-site accessibility as inspiration, not as an audited guarantee.
